# LIT — do scale-rich (SR) partitions occur in living systems?

Literature scan, 2026-11-15. Question: is there a biological planar partition built by
"nucleate, grow both ways, stop at the first obstacle", so that all junctions are
T-junctions, older segments are thicker, and the dual graph (segments = nodes,
T-contacts = edges) has p_k ~ k^-3, C(k) ~ 1/k, k ∝ length × age?

How much was read, per entry:

fetch tool · **[ABS]** abstract only · **[SEC]** search snippets or secondary citation
only; verify before relying on it · **[MEM]** my prior knowledge (citation verified,
content not).

## 0. Bottom line

1. **No published junction census** (T vs X vs Y), segment-length distribution or dual
   degree distribution exists for stress-fibre networks of flat cells. No paper reports a
   power-law degree distribution for an image-derived cytoskeletal network.
2. **Close prior art exists.** Random tessellations have been fitted to cytoskeleton:
   keratin with Poisson line/Voronoi/Delaunay (Beil 2006); lamellipodial actin with
   multilayer tessellations (Fleischer 2007); stress fibres as a Poisson segment process
   with intersection interactions (Beneš 2017). Cortical actin compartments are
   log-normal, read as *repeated fragmentation by growing filaments*, with d_f = 1.75
   (Sadegh 2017, PRX). STIT, Gilbert and Cowan models have **not** been applied to
   cytoskeleton or tissue images (none found).
3. **Actin is a mixed-junction system.** Y junctions: Arp2/3, 70°, one-sided. T-like:
   dorsal fibres growing from adhesions and ending on arcs. X: arcs over dorsal fibres,
   and the cortical mesh. Multi-armed vertices: Lazarides polygonal networks.
   Thickening comes from **lateral fusion and coalescence**. That still gives
   "older = thicker" for arcs (Tojkander 2015), but by a different mechanism from
   λ_t ∝ t^-α.
4. **The cleanest biological T-insertion process is plant cytokinesis.** The cell plate
   grows centrifugally until it fuses with parental walls, and four-way junctions are
   actively avoided. **Hierarchical leaf venation** is the cleanest case with a thickness
   hierarchy, and public images exist.
5. **Fracture-network precedent for the dual graph.** Andresen et al. 2013 built the
   "fractures = nodes" dual for rock outcrops and found broad, roughly log-normal degree
   distributions that are disassortative.

## 1. Actin in flat cells as a planar partition

### 1.1 Architecture and junction types (qualitative)

- **Hotulainen & Lappalainen 2006** [ABS + SEC]. "Stress fibers are generated by two
  distinct actin assembly mechanisms in motile cells", *J Cell Biol* 173, 383–394.
  doi:10.1083/jcb.200511093.
  - Dorsal SFs: one end at a focal adhesion (FA); they elongate by formin (mDia1/DRF1)
    polymerisation at the FA, i.e. **one-sided growth from an anchor**.
  - Transverse arcs: *endwise annealing* of short Arp2/3-nucleated actin bundles and
    myosin bundles; not anchored.
  - Dorsal SFs plus arcs convert into ventral SFs (anchored at both ends).
- **Tojkander et al. 2015** [FT-part]. "Generation of contractile actomyosin bundles
  depends on mechanosensitive actin filament assembly and disassembly", *eLife* 4,
  e06126. doi:10.7554/eLife.06126.
  - Quotes: "Transverse arcs are typically connected to several focal adhesion-attached
    dorsal stress fibers along their length"; "multiple connections points, thus forming
    a spider-net-like structure".
  - Arcs "undergo lateral fusion during their centripetal flow" into thicker bundles.
    Fusion often starts where arcs meet elongating dorsal SFs.
  - Dorsal SFs not connected to arcs disassemble (ADF/cofilin pruning).
  - So older means thicker, by **fusion**. The text does not say whether dorsal SFs end
    on arcs (T) or pass under them (X).
- **Burnette et al. 2014** [ABS + SEC]. "A contractile and counterbalancing adhesion
  system controls the 3D shape of crawling cells", *J Cell Biol* 205, 83–96.
  doi:10.1083/jcb.201311104 [DOI MEM; PMC3987145]. The arc meshwork is sarcomere-like and
  coupled to non-contractile DSFs that pivot on FAs. Three lamellar classes: arcs, DSFs,
  ventral SFs.
- **Burnette et al. 2011** [ABS + SEC]. "A role for actin arcs in the leading-edge advance
  of migrating cells", *Nat Cell Biol* 13, 371–382. doi:10.1038/ncb2205. Myosin II
  *condenses* the lamellipodial network into arcs. An arc is not a single nucleated
  segment.
- **Lehtimäki et al. 2021** [ABS + SEC]. "Generation of stress fibers through
  myosin-driven reorganization of the actin cortex", *eLife* 10, e60710.
  doi:10.7554/eLife.60710 (correction doi:10.7554/eLife.79304). Myosin pulses reorganise
  the cortical mesh into "cortical stress fibres" (mostly under the nucleus);
  mDia1–3-independent.
- **Vignaud et al. 2021** [ABS]. "Stress fibres are embedded in a contractile cortical
  network", *Nat Mater* 20, 410–420. doi:10.1038/s41563-020-00825-z.
  - Bundles are embedded along their full length in a continuous contractile cortical
    mesh (ablation + traction force microscopy + cryo-ET).
  - They "appeared to originate from the alignment and coalescence of thin and unattached
    cortical actin filaments".
  - So the ventral network is **bundle-in-mesh**, not a partition by isolated segments.
- **Lazarides 1976** [ABS + SEC]. "Actin, α-actinin, and tropomyosin interaction in the
  structural organization of actin filaments in nonmuscle cells", *J Cell Biol* 68,
  202–219. doi:10.1083/jcb.68.2.202 [DOI MEM]. About 40% of spreading rat embryo cells
  form polygonal "geodesic" networks: α-actinin foci with several arms (not T), which act
  as organising centres for later bundles.
- **Khatau et al. 2009** [ABS + SEC]. "A perinuclear actin cap regulates nuclear shape",
  *PNAS* 106, 19017–19022 [pages MEM]. doi:10.1073/pnas.0908686106. A dome of thick,
  roughly parallel contractile bundles held by LINC complexes; few junctions.
- **Tee et al. 2015** [MEM, citation verified]. "Cellular chirality arising from the
  self-organization of the actin cytoskeleton", *Nat Cell Biol* 17, 445–457.
  doi:10.1038/ncb3137. On circular islands, radial fibres grow from FAs and transverse
  fibres flow inward along them: a radial × azimuthal grid, more woven lattice than
  random T partition.
- **Kassianidou et al. 2017** [ABS]. "Geometry and network connectivity govern the
  mechanics of stress fibers", *PNAS* 114, 2622–2627. doi:10.1073/pnas.1606649114.
  Retraction after ablation depends on how integrated an SF is with its neighbours;
  connectivity is not counted as a graph.
- **Lamellipodium (Arp2/3).**
  - **Svitkina & Borisy 1999** [ABS + SEC]. *J Cell Biol* 145, 1009–1026.
    doi:10.1083/jcb.145.5.1009. Pointed ends sit at Arp2/3 **Y-junctions**, ~67–70°.
  - **Mueller et al. 2017** [ABS]. "Load adaptation of lamellipodial actin networks",
    *Cell* 171, 188–200. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2017.07.051.
  - **Fäßler et al. 2020** [ABS]. *Nat Commun* 11, 6437. doi:10.1038/s41467-020-20286-x.
    Structural; no segment statistics.
  - Arp2/3 branching is one-sided with a fixed angle. It is topologically distinct from
    SR's two-way growth ending on *other* segments.

### 1.2 Quantitative statistics that do exist

- **Sadegh et al. 2017** [FT, grep]. "Plasma membrane is compartmentalized by a
  self-similar cortical actin meshwork", *Phys Rev X* 7, 011031.
  doi:10.1103/PhysRevX.7.011031.
  - HEK293, STORM, watershed compartments (9 cells, n = 2,500): areas fit a
    **log-normal** (σ = 0.8).
  - Quote: "actin-delimited compartments are split into smaller compartments by growing
    actin filaments and thus their distribution is predicted to be log-normal"
    (Kolmogorov breakage).
  - Box-counting **d_f = 1.75 ± 0.02**, attributed to Arp2/3 branching.
  - The closest published "iterated division by growing filaments" picture of actin.
    Never tested against STIT or Cowan statistics.
- **Morone et al. 2006** [FT-part]. *J Cell Biol* 174, 851–862.
  doi:10.1083/jcb.200606007. Membrane-skeleton mesh by electron tomography: median √area
  200 nm (NRK) and 52 nm (FRSK). Histograms only; no fitted form, junction types or
  segment lengths. The mesh matches hop-diffusion compartments.
- **Fleischer et al. 2007** [ABS + SEC]. "Actin network architecture and elasticity in
  lamellipodia of melanoma cells", *New J Phys* 9, 420.
  doi:10.1088/1367-2630/9/11/420. Two-layer superpositions of Poisson line, Voronoi and
  Delaunay tessellations fitted to EM of B16F1 lamellipodia; mesh sizes 34 and 78 nm.
  Degree statistics: full text not reached.
- **Beneš et al. 2017** [ABS + SEC]. "Estimation of parameters in a planar segment
  process with a biological application", *Image Anal Stereol* 36, 25–33.
  doi:10.5566/ias.1627. Gibbs segment processes with interactions through the number of
  *intersections* (model I) or a length distribution plus radial trend (model II).
  Fitted to hMSC stress fibres; "quite satisfactory". Fibres overlap (X allowed), with no
  T structure.
- **Per-fibre length and width, no graph statistics.**
  - **SFEX: Zhang, Xia & Kanchanawong 2017** [ABS + SEC]. *BMC Bioinformatics* 18, 268
    [art. no. MEM]. doi:10.1186/s12859-017-1684-y. U2OS on Y, crossbow and disc
    micropatterns; ventral SF length and width; "branching" regions marked; no census.
  - **FilamentSensor: Eltzner et al. 2015** [ABS + SEC]. *PLoS ONE* 10, e0126346.
    doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0126346. **Public hMSC benchmark images with expert-marked
    fibres.**
  - **FilamentSensor 2.0: Hauke et al. 2023** [ABS + SEC]. *PLoS ONE* 18, e0279336.
    doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0279336. Single-fibre tracking over time → fibre ages.
  - **Alioscha-Perez et al. 2016** [ABS]. *PLoS Comput Biol* 12, e1005063.
    doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005063. Quasi-straight fibres in osteoblasts; no junction
    statistics.
  - **Lichtenstein, Geiger & Kam 2003** [SEC]. *Cytometry A* 54, 8–18.
    doi:10.1002/cyto.a.10053. "FiberScore": an orientation score, not a graph.
- **Graph tools that report junctions.**
  - **SOAX: Xu et al. 2015** [SEC]. *Sci Rep* 5, 9081. doi:10.1038/srep09081.
    **Warning:** snakes stop when "their tip collides with the body of another SOAC to
    form a T-junction". **T-junctions are produced by the algorithm**, so any T share
    from collision-terminated tracers is biased toward SR.
  - **FiNTA: Flormann et al. 2021** [SEC]. *FASEB J* 35(5), e21582 [art. no. MEM].
    doi:10.1096/fj.202100048R. Reports junction coordination number, junction distance
    and hole area; values not retrieved.
  - **DeFiNe: Breuer & Nikoloski 2015** [ABS + SEC]. *Sci Rep* 5, 18267.
    doi:10.1038/srep18267. Disentangles a junction graph back into filaments: **the step
    needed to build the SR dual**.
  - **Breuer et al. 2014** [ABS]. *J R Soc Interface* 11, 20140362.
    doi:10.1098/rsif.2014.0362. Grid-based graphs of plant actin and MTs; their "degree"
    is an intensity sum, **not** a junction degree.
  - **Gan et al. 2016** [MEM, citation verified]. *Cell Syst* 3, 252–263.
    doi:10.1016/j.cels.2016.08.007. Vimentin and MT reconstruction; no degree statistics
    known.
- **Keratin intermediate filaments (the best-quantified cytoskeletal graph).**
  - **Beil et al. 2005** [ABS]. *J Microsc* 220, 84–95.
    doi:10.1111/j.1365-2818.2005.01505.x. SEM skeleton → graph.
  - **Beil et al. 2006** [ABS]. "Fitting of random tessellation models to keratin
    filament networks", *J Theor Biol* 241, 62–72. doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2005.11.009.
    Model selection among Poisson line, Poisson–Voronoi and Poisson–Delaunay.
  - **Lück et al. 2010** [FT, grep]. *J Microsc* 239, 1–16.
    doi:10.1111/j.1365-2818.2009.03348.x. Mean vertex degree **3.5–4.2** (SD 0.23) over
    11 Panc-1 tomograms; degree 4 and ≥ 5 common; mean segment length anti-correlated with
    degree.
  - Lück et al., "Statistical analysis of the intermediate filament network in cells of
    mesenchymal lineage…" [FT, grep; Ulm preprint, journal version not checked]: mean
    edge length and density, periphery vs perinuclear.
  - **Not T-dominated:** a pure T partition has mean degree 3, and crossings push it
    toward 4.

### 1.3 Assessment for Q1

- **Junctions are a mixture**, with no published proportions:
  - lamellipodium: Arp2/3 Y;
  - lamella: dorsal SF ends on arcs (T-like) and crossings (X);
  - ventral: bundles in a cortical mesh;
  - spreading cells: multi-armed vertices.
- **Thickness vs age:** arcs thicken by lateral fusion as they flow centripetally, so
  distance from the edge is an age proxy. Ventral and cortical SFs thicken by
  coalescence. Thickness reflects **merging**, not elongation time.
- **Pore sizes:** log-normal (cortex, STORM); histograms only (EM); no power law reported.

## 2. Dynamics: does anything "nucleate, grow both ways, stop at the first obstacle"?

### Actin
- **Dorsal SF** (Tojkander 2015 [FT-part]).
  - Grows **one way** from an FA at "∼0.25 μm/min".
  - Intact ventral SFs elongate at ~0.02 µm/min, and ~0.23 µm/min after ablation.
  - SF actin turns over with t½ ≈ 1 min (FRAP): long-lived bundles, fast-exchanging
    subunits.
- **Arcs.**
  - **Heath 1983** [SEC]. *J Cell Sci* 60, 331–354. doi:10.1242/jcs.60.1.331. Arcs form
    2–10 µm behind the edge and flow centripetally at ~1.3 µm/min (chick fibroblasts).
  - They are assembled by annealing and condensation (Hotulainen 2006; Burnette 2011),
    not by two-ended tip growth.
- **Formin single filaments.**
  - **Higashida et al. 2004** [ABS]. *Science* 303, 2007–2010.
    doi:10.1126/science.1093923. mDia1 moves processively "for tens of microns". The
    ~2 µm/s figure is [MEM, unverified].
  - Filaments cross a cell in seconds (Π ≪ 1 at the filament scale), but they are not
    resolved optically, and visible bundles form by coalescence.
- **Arp2/3:** one-sided 70° branching off a mother. Not SR.
- **Π = r ℓ³ / v for SF bundles.**
  - v ≈ 0.25 µm/min and ℓ ≈ 10–30 µm give a crossing time of ~40–120 min.
  - Bundle turnover is minutes to tens of minutes.
  - **No published nucleation rate r** (new SF initiations per µm² per min) was found.
    Measure it from LifeAct movies (e.g. with FilamentSensor 2.0 tracks).
  - Expected regime: Π ≳ 1 with turnover comparable to the crossing time, i.e.
    Gilbert-with-turnover, not sequential SR.

### Keratin intermediate filaments (cleanest nucleate–elongate–flow–mature cycle)
- **Kölsch et al. 2010** [ABS]. "The keratin-filament cycle of assembly and disassembly",
  *J Cell Sci* 123, 2266–2272. doi:10.1242/jcs.068080. Assembly happens in the periphery;
  new filaments are transported inward while disassembly occurs; juxtanuclear filaments
  stabilise into a cage. Older filaments are central and bundled.
- **Windoffer et al. 2011** [SEC]. *J Cell Biol* 194, 669–678.
  doi:10.1083/jcb.201008095. Nucleation (near FAs) → elongation → transport →
  maturation/bundling or disassembly.
- **Werner et al. 2004** [FT-part]. *Mol Biol Cell* 15, 990–1002.
  doi:10.1091/mbc.E03-09-0687. Aggregates flow inward at 0.6 µm/min, have t½ < 15 min,
  and grow with distance from the edge.
- **Beil et al. 2009** [ABS]. *J Theor Biol* 256, 518–532.
  doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2008.09.044.
  - A stochastic nucleation/elongation/branching/annealing model; architecture "mostly
    depends on the balance between filament elongation and branching", and annealing
    sets the mesh-size distribution.
  - The closest existing *process* model to a parallel Gilbert/SR relaxation. But the
    measured degree is ~4 (§1.2).

### Microtubules (collision outcomes measured directly)
- **Dixit & Cyr 2004** [ABS + SEC]. *Plant Cell* 16, 3274–3284.
  doi:10.1105/tpc.104.026930.
  - When a growing plus-end meets an existing MT: shallow angles (≲ 40°) → zippering;
    steep angles → crossover or induced catastrophe. Steep collisions are "twice as
    likely to result in microtubule depolymerization", and crossover is the most frequent
    steep outcome per secondary sources.
  - Mostly **X**, with a minority of "stop at first hit".
  - **The only system with a measured T-vs-X branching ratio per collision.**

### Plant cytokinesis (literal two-way growth to the first wall)
- The cell plate and phragmoplast start in the centre and expand centrifugally until
  fusing with parental walls. In 2D section this is a segment growing both ways and
  ending on existing walls: **T-junctions by construction**.
- **Nishihama & Machida 2001** [SEC]. *Curr Opin Plant Biol* 4, 507–512.
  doi:10.1016/S1369-5266(00)00208-9.
- **Smertenko 2018** [citation only]. *Curr Opin Plant Biol* 46, 130–137.
  doi:10.1016/j.pbi.2018.07.011.

## 3. Other biological candidates

### 3.1 Plant tissue (iterated division with T-insertion)
- **Besson & Dumais 2011** [ABS]. "Universal rule for the symmetric division of plant
  cells", *PNAS* 108, 6294–6299. doi:10.1073/pnas.1011866108. The plane is chosen among
  local area minima with Gibbs probability ∝ exp(−β·relative area). **Not a random
  angle;** the rule is closer to "shortest chord through the centre" than to Cowan's
  isotropic rule.
- **Four-way junction avoidance.**
  - **Gascon et al. 2025** [ABS]. *Curr Biol* 35, 2789–2801.
    doi:10.1016/j.cub.2025.04.046. Cell plates are guided near, but offset from, the
    adjacent tricellular junction. A SAC9 mutant attaches equidistant from it; perturbed
    mechanics → four-way junctions.
  - **Müller 2025** [ABS]. Commentary, *Curr Biol* 35, R608–R610.
    doi:10.1016/j.cub.2025.04.020.
  - **Wang et al. 2024** [ABS]. *Cell Rep* 43, 114844.
    doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2024.114844. Actin mutants → more four-way junctions and
    rectangular cells; the internal angles at tricellular junctions change over time.
  - T-junctions **relax** after insertion, so straight-segment identity is lost.
- **Jackson et al. 2019** [ABS]. *Cell Syst* 8, 53–65. doi:10.1016/j.cels.2018.12.009.
  Live 3D SAM cell-adjacency networks; division planes limit daughters' shortest-path
  load. Cells are the nodes, not walls.
- No wall-thickness vs age data and no wall-dual degree statistics found. These need wall
  lineage (MorphoGraphX time-lapse).

### 3.2 Animal epithelia
- **Gibson et al. 2006** [ABS]. *Nature* 442, 1038–1041. doi:10.1038/nature05014.
  Division (a T-like insertion of a new edge) gives a fixed polygon distribution across
  metazoa. T1 remodelling erases segment identity, and there is no thickness hierarchy.
  **Poor candidate.**

### 3.3 Leaf venation (hierarchy + T-junctions + thickness)
- **Couder et al. 2002** [ABS + SEC]. *Eur Phys J B* 28, 135–138.
  doi:10.1140/epjb/e2002-00211-1. Gel-film cracks reproduce venation patterns; the
  topology fits growth in a tensorial stress field.
- **Bohn et al. 2002** [ABS + SEC]. "Constitutive property of the local organization of
  leaf venation networks", *Phys Rev E* 65, 061914. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.65.061914.
  Junction angles are fixed by vein radii (a force-balance analogy). A thin vein meets a
  thick one at ≈ 90° (**T**); equal veins meet at ≈ 120°. Universal across the species
  studied.
- **Laguna, Bohn & Jagla 2008** [SEC]. *PLoS Comput Biol* 4, e1000055.
  doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000055.
- **Corson, Adda-Bedia & Boudaoud 2009** [SEC]. *J Theor Biol* 259, 440–448.
  doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2009.05.002.
- **Ronellenfitsch et al. 2015** [ABS + SEC]. *PLoS Comput Biol* 11, e1004680.
  doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004680. Nested-loop hierarchy trees for 186 leaves /
  137 species; developmental noise explains the topological traits.
- **Katifori, Szöllősi & Magnasco 2010** [citation verified]. *PRL* 104, 048704.
  doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.048704.
- **Blonder et al. 2020** [SEC]. *New Phytol* 228, 1796–1810. doi:10.1111/nph.16830.
  Segmented networks of 260 species; Oxford ORA data record. Whether per-segment widths
  are included was **not checked**.
- **Order and age:** major veins form first and are widest; minor orders subdivide the
  areoles later (Couder's crack picture).
- **No dual degree distribution** (vein = node) found.

### 3.4 Physical cracking in skin
- **Milinkovitch et al. 2013** [ABS + SEC]. *Science* 339, 78–81.
  doi:10.1126/science.1226265. Crocodile head scales are random polygons from cracking
  of keratinised skin under growth stress. Many **incomplete edges**; areas do not fit
  foam statistics. Junction census and hierarchy are not in the abstract (check the SI).

### 3.5 Hierarchical cracks (non-living reference class)
- **Bohn, Pauchard & Couder 2005** [SEC]. *Phys Rev E* 71, 046214.
  doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.71.046214. The genealogical division tree can be read back from
  the final geometry.
- **Bohn et al. 2005** [citation only]. Part II, *Phys Rev E* 71, 046215.
  doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.71.046215.
- **Bohn, Douady & Couder 2005** [ABS via aggregator]. *PRL* 94, 054503.
  doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.94.054503. Mean number of sides = 4, generic for successive
  division with no reorganisation; conserved "geometrical charge" n − 4. **A ready-made
  discriminator: ⟨n⟩ = 4 for T-partitions, 6 for Y/Voronoi.**
- **Nagel, Mecke, Ohser & Weiss 2008** [SEC]. *Image Anal Stereol* 27, 73–78.
  doi:10.5566/ias.v27.p73-78. STIT fits real cracks poorly; a modified model is proposed.
- **Andresen et al. 2013** [FT-part]. "Topology of fracture networks", *Front Phys* 1, 7.
  doi:10.3389/fphy.2013.00007. **The SR dual graph, already done for rock fractures.**
  - Fracture = node; crossing or abutting = link.
  - Outcrops: a "broad degree distribution", fitted by log-normal (a log-log fit gives
    ~ −2.3); small-world per the abstract (the excerpt read does not repeat it);
    **disassortative**.
  - DFN models are assortative. No T vs X split, no C(k), no degree–length analysis.

### 3.6 Not pursued: weak fit or nothing found
- **Insect wings.** **Hoffmann et al. 2018** [ABS]. *PNAS* 115, 9905–9910.
  doi:10.1073/pnas.1721248115. Odonata secondary veins come from inhibitory centres
  (Voronoi-like), not cracks.
- **Bone trabeculae, fungal hyphae, Physarum:** no T-insertion partition statistics
  found; 3D, loop-forming or fusion-based; not searched further.

## 5. Ranked candidates for a quantitative test

**1. Hierarchical leaf venation (minor veins).**
- Why: T junctions follow from the Bohn radius–angle rule; width encodes order and age;
  crack-like successive division; public segmented networks (Blonder 2020,
  Ronellenfitsch 2015).
- Published: angle–radius rule, loop-nesting trees, vein density.
- New analysis needed:
  - merge collinear pieces across junctions into segments (DeFiNe-like);
  - build the dual graph and get p_k, C(k), k vs length × width;
  - measure the T/X/Y share and areole ⟨n⟩ (4 vs 6).
- Caveat: loops and reconnection; freely ending veinlets.

**2. Arabidopsis SAM / epidermis with lineage (MorphoGraphX time-lapse).**
- Why: the cell plate literally grows both ways to the first wall; four-way junctions
  are avoided; wall ages are known exactly.
- Published: division-plane rules, junction-avoidance mechanics, cell-adjacency networks.
- New analysis needed: track each wall's original identity through later T-insertions,
  build the wall dual, and get k vs age.
- Caveats: Errera/Gibbs plane choice rather than random area or angle; no thickness
  hierarchy; T relaxation and growth stretch old walls.

**3. Actin stress-fibre networks of flat cells (U2OS, fibroblasts, hMSC; the primary
target).**
- Why: arcs thicken with age by fusion; dorsal SFs end on arcs (T-like); FilamentSensor
  benchmark images and SFEX micropattern data exist.
- Published: per-fibre length, width and orientation; segment-process fits with
  crossings; SF actin t½ ~ 1 min; dorsal elongation 0.25 µm/min.
- New analysis needed, all of it:
  - a junction census with a tracer that does **not** force T's;
